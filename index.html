Buying Guides

Before You Choose Your Grooming Gear

Product specifications only tell part of the story. Think about how you actually groom before deciding which equipment makes sense for you.

GUIDE 01

Trimmer vs. Electric Shaver

A trimmer is generally intended to leave and shape facial hair at selected lengths, while an electric shaver is intended for a closer shave. Your preferred facial-hair length should influence which tool you choose.

GUIDE 02

How Many Attachments Do You Need?

More attachments are useful only when you actually need them. Someone trimming a beard at one consistent length may need far fewer accessories than someone grooming beard, hair and body.

GUIDE 03

Battery Runtime Matters

Long cordless runtime can be convenient for travel and for people who do not want to recharge frequently. Consider runtime together with charging time and whether the device can be used while plugged in.

GUIDE 04

Think About Replacement Parts

Before choosing a grooming tool, consider whether replacement blades, guards and other consumable parts are readily available and what they may cost over time.

GUIDE 05

Wet vs. Dry Grooming

Some grooming devices are designed for wet and dry use while others have different water-resistance limitations. Always check the manufacturer's instructions before using or cleaning a device with water.

GUIDE 06

Choosing a Shaving Product

Shaving creams and gels vary in texture, lubrication, fragrance and skin feel. Your shaving method and skin preferences can be more important than simply choosing the most heavily advertised product.

GUIDE 07

Travel-Friendly Grooming

For travel, consider the size of the device, battery life, charging equipment and how many separate attachments you realistically need to carry.

GUIDE 08

Cleaning Your Grooming Equipment

Hair and grooming residue can accumulate around blades and attachments. Follow the manufacturer's cleaning instructions and allow washable components to dry properly before storage.

GUIDE 09

Beard Length and Guard Selection

Different guard lengths produce different results. Starting with a longer guard and gradually moving shorter can provide more control when trying a new style.

GUIDE 10

Value Is More Than Purchase Price

Compare the initial price with included accessories, replacement-part costs, expected usage and whether one device can replace several grooming tools.
